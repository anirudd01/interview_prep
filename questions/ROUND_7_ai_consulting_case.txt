=====================================================================
ROUND 7 - AI CONSULTING & SOLUTIONS ARCHITECTURE (CASE ROUND)
"Can he sit with a business owner, find where AI actually pays off,
 design something that works, and say no when it doesn't?"
Duration: 45 min case + 15 min rapid probes
=====================================================================

WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
Rounds 1-6 test whether he can BUILD. This round tests whether he can ADVISE: the
skill that separates an AI engineer from an AI solutions architect, forward-deployed
engineer or AI consultant (see plan/AI_CONSULTANT_ROADMAP.md, sections 2 and 5 Layer 3).
Real interviews for these roles include a customer role-play or case: the interviewer
plays a client with a vague AI wish, and the candidate has to run discovery, frame the
problem, size it, propose a solution with risks and costs, and land a next step.

Status: introduced 2026-10-14. Not expected to go well before Phase 1 of the roadmap
(Feb 2027+). Use section G cases sparingly before then. Sections A-C and H are fair
game earlier, as practice.

HOW TO RUN THIS ROUND
- Default format: ONE case from section G (30-45 min), then 4-6 rapid probes from
  A-F or H (10-15 min).
- In the case, YOU PLAY THE CLIENT. Stay in character. Be vague at first ("we want to
  use AI in underwriting"). Only reveal facts (volumes, costs, systems, data, pain)
  when he asks good discovery questions. If he jumps straight to architecture without
  asking anything, let him, and then grade it down hard.
- Have numbers ready to reveal when asked. Each case lists "facts to reveal on
  request". Invent consistent extra numbers if he asks something not listed.
- Push back like a real client: "that sounds expensive", "my CTO says we should just
  buy Copilot", "can it be 100% accurate?", "we need it live in 6 weeks".
- In voice mode, keep your client lines short. Let him drive.

GRADING RUBRIC (score each 1-4: 1 = missing, 2 = shallow, 3 = solid, 4 = strong)
  1. Discovery     - asked about users, current process, volumes, cost of errors,
                     data, systems, constraints, success criteria BEFORE proposing
  2. Framing       - restated the real problem in business terms with a baseline
                     ("40 min per submission, 3 FTE, 30% rework")
  3. Sizing        - did the value math AND the cost math (tokens, infra, people),
                     even roughly, out loud
  4. Solution      - proportionate design (not over-engineered); knew where AI is
                     the wrong tool; human-in-the-loop where errors are expensive
  5. Evaluation    - defined how success is measured: eval set, metrics, acceptance
                     thresholds, BEFORE building
  6. Risk          - security (prompt injection, data leakage), compliance, vendor
                     lock-in, failure modes, what happens when the model is wrong
  7. Delivery      - phased plan (discovery -> PoC -> pilot -> production), with
                     decision gates and an honest timeline
  8. Communication - structured (answer first), checked in with the client, handled
                     pushback calmly, ended with a concrete next step
Pass bar for an AI SA / FDE role: no score below 2, average >= 3.

RED FLAGS
- Proposes a solution in the first 2 minutes ("we'll build a RAG with an agent").
- Never asks what an error costs, or never asks for the current baseline.
- No evaluation plan, or "we'll test it with users" as the only plan.
- Promises accuracy numbers without having seen the data.
- Can't say when NOT to use an LLM.
- Doesn't mention data access, permissions or PII until prompted.
- Says yes to everything the client asks for, including impossible timelines.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
A. DISCOVERY & PROBLEM FRAMING
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  A client says "we want to use AI". What are the first ten questions you ask, and
    in what order? Why that order?
2.  How do you measure the baseline of a manual process before you build anything?
    What happens to your project if you skip this?
3.  Who do you need in the room for a discovery workshop, and what does each person
    give you? What happens if the actual end users aren't there?
4.  Walk me through how you'd map an underwriter's submission-handling process. What
    artefact do you produce at the end?
5.  The executive sponsor and the end users describe the problem differently. How do
    you reconcile it, and whose version wins?
6.  How do you tell a "needs AI" problem from a "needs a form / an API / a rules
    engine / a process change" problem? Give two examples from your own work where
    the right answer wasn't AI.
7.  What does "the cost of being wrong" mean for an AI use case, and how does it
    change your design? Compare email triage vs claims denial.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
B. USE-CASE PRIORITISATION & BUSINESS CASE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
8.  A client hands you a list of 15 AI ideas. How do you prioritise them? Show me
    your scoring dimensions and how you'd weight them.
9.  Build the business case out loud: 3 underwriting assistants spend 40% of their
    time re-keying broker submissions into the rating system. Loaded cost is
    GBP 55k/year each. What's the value, what's the cost, and what's the payback?
10. Do the token cost math: 8,000 documents a day, an average of 6 pages each, about
    700 tokens per page in and 1,500 tokens out per document. What's the monthly
    model cost at roughly USD 3 per million input tokens and USD 15 per million
    output tokens? What three levers cut it the most?
11. Your PoC hit 85% field accuracy. The business case assumed 95%. Walk me through
    what happens to the ROI and what you tell the sponsor.
12. What costs do people forget when they build an AI business case? Name at least
    five beyond model tokens.
13. "Quick win" vs "strategic bet": give one of each for a specialty insurer, and
    explain why you'd do them in that order.
14. How do you measure value AFTER go-live? What do you instrument on day one?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
C. BUILD vs BUY vs PARTNER, AND ARCHITECTURE RECOMMENDATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
15. The CTO says "just give everyone Microsoft Copilot / ChatGPT Enterprise". When
    is that the right answer, and when isn't it?
16. How do you evaluate an AI vendor that claims 98% extraction accuracy for
    insurance documents? What do you ask for, and what test do you run?
17. Build vs buy for document extraction: give me the decision criteria and the
    point at which you'd switch from one to the other.
18. Which model provider and hosting model would you recommend for a UK insurer with
    data-residency concerns? What are the options, and the trade-offs of each?
19. When do you recommend self-hosting an open-weight model? Give the numbers that
    would tip the decision.
20. Lock-in: how do you design an AI system so the client can switch model provider
    in a week?
21. A client wants a "multi-agent platform". What questions do you ask, and what do
    you probably end up recommending instead?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
D. PoC -> PILOT -> PRODUCTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------
22. Why do most AI PoCs never reach production? Give five reasons and the
    countermeasure for each.
23. Define the success criteria for an extraction PoC before it starts. Be specific:
    metrics, thresholds, dataset, and who signs off.
24. How do you build an evaluation set with the client's subject-matter experts when
    they have no time? How big does it need to be?
25. Design a pilot for an underwriting assistant: which users, for how long, shadow
    mode or live, what you measure, and what the go/no-go gate is.
26. What's on your production-readiness checklist for an LLM feature? At least eight
    items.
27. The model provider silently updates the model and accuracy drops 6 points. How
    would you have caught it, and what's the process now?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
E. GOVERNANCE, RISK & COMPLIANCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
28. How does the EU AI Act classify AI used in insurance? Which uses are high-risk,
    and what obligations follow? (Be honest about which dates and details you'd
    re-check.)
29. A UK insurer asks: "what do we need in place before we use AI in underwriting
    decisions?" Give a practical answer: model inventory, risk assessment, human
    oversight, explainability, monitoring, audit trail, Consumer Duty fair outcomes.
30. NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001: what are they, and when would a client actually
    need one?
31. Personal data in prompts: what are the controls? Think vendor terms, retention,
    redaction, residency, access logging.
32. Indirect prompt injection in an inbox-processing agent (your Orbit/Ayana
    pipeline): explain the risk to a non-technical risk committee in 60 seconds, then
    give the mitigations.
33. How do you design human oversight so it's real, and not a rubber stamp
    ("automation bias")?
34. A declined applicant asks why. What does your system need to have recorded to
    answer that?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
F. STAKEHOLDERS, CHANGE & DIFFICULT CONVERSATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
35. The sponsor wants it live in 6 weeks. You think it needs 4 months. How do you run
    that conversation?
36. Underwriters don't trust the AI summaries and keep redoing the work. Adoption is
    20%. What do you do?
37. Tell me about a time you told a stakeholder "no" or "not like that" and they
    agreed. (STAR; push for a real story.)
38. How do you present a PoC that partly failed? Structure the 10-minute readout.
39. Write a one-paragraph executive summary, out loud, recommending NOT to proceed
    with a use case.
40. How do you hand over an AI system to a client team so it survives after you
    leave?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
G. FULL CASES (pick ONE per session; interviewer plays the client)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
41. CASE: "AI for submissions." A Lloyd's MGA receives broker submissions by email.
    They want "AI to speed up underwriting".
    Facts to reveal on request:
    - 1,200 submissions/month; about 35% are declined after a full review.
    - Underwriting assistants spend about 45 min re-keying each submission.
    - Formats: email body, PDF slips, Excel schedules of values, sometimes scans.
    - Rating happens in an in-house rater; there's an API, but it's undocumented.
    - Errors in limits or deductibles have caused mispriced risks before.
    - Data sits in Outlook and SharePoint; IT is Azure-first; there's a 3-person
      dev team.
    Strong answer: triage first (decline faster), then extraction with human review
    on money fields, eval set from historical submissions, Azure-hosted models,
    phased plan, ROI from both time saved and faster declines.

42. CASE: "Chat with our policies." A carrier wants staff to ask questions about
    5,000 policy wordings and 300 underwriting guidelines.
    Facts to reveal on request:
    - 400 users, across 4 business units with different permissions.
    - Documents are PDFs, many scanned, versioned, some superseded.
    - Wrong answers on coverage could lead to a mis-sold policy.
    - They already pay for Microsoft 365 E5.
    Strong answer: question whether M365 Copilot/SharePoint search covers it first;
    if building, permission-aware RAG, version filtering, citations and refusals,
    an eval set written with underwriters, and a pilot with one business unit.

43. CASE: "Replace our rater spreadsheets." (Your Cellular experience.) An insurer has
    60 Excel raters that drive pricing. The CIO read about AI agents and wants "an
    agent that prices risks".
    Facts to reveal on request:
    - Raters change quarterly; the actuaries own them.
    - Regulators and auditors need reproducible prices.
    - Two raters have macros; one has circular references.
    Strong answer: pricing must be deterministic, so convert the raters to tested
    code with output-equivalence tests; use LLMs only for the conversion assist,
    explanations and data gathering, never for computing the price; plus a
    governance and audit trail.

44. CASE: "Claims FNOL." A personal-lines insurer wants AI to handle first
    notification of loss by phone and web.
    Facts to reveal on request:
    - 20k claims/month; peaks of 5x after storms.
    - 15% of claims are flagged for fraud review; the fraud model is a separate
      gradient-boosted model.
    - Vulnerable customers are a regulatory focus.
    Strong answer: intake and structuring with AI, keep the fraud model, routing and
    escalation for vulnerable customers, surge-capacity design, and a Consumer Duty
    lens.

45. CASE: "Bordereaux chaos." A syndicate receives monthly bordereaux spreadsheets
    from 40 coverholders, each in a different format. Reconciliation takes 2 people
    a full week each month.
    Facts to reveal on request:
    - Standards exist (e.g. Lloyd's coverholder reporting standards), but adoption
      is uneven.
    - Errors lead to wrong premium and claims reporting.
    Strong answer: schema mapping per coverholder (LLM-assisted mapping, then
    deterministic transforms), validation rules, anomaly flags, a human approval
    workflow, and a learning loop that reuses mappings.

46. CASE (generic, non-insurance): "Customer support AI." A SaaS company with 50k
    tickets a month wants to "cut support costs by 50% with AI".
    Strong answer: analyse the ticket mix first, deflect via self-serve and docs,
    agent-assist before automation, measure resolution quality not just
    deflection, and staged autonomy by ticket category.

47. CASE (internal): "Our AI pilot is stuck." (Role-play the sponsor.) Your company
    built a RAG assistant 6 months ago. Usage is low, nobody trusts it, and the
    team wants a bigger model. Diagnose it and propose a turnaround plan.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
H. RAPID AI ESTIMATION & COST DRILLS (60-90s each, no calculator)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
48. 1M tokens is about how many English words, and about how many pages?
49. A chatbot has 2,000 daily users x 10 messages x 3k tokens of context. What's the
    daily token volume? What does prompt caching change?
50. Embedding 5M chunks of 500 tokens each: total tokens, and a rough cost at about
    USD 0.02 per million tokens?
51. A GPU serves a 7B model at about 1,500 output tokens/sec aggregate. How many GPUs
    do you need for 200 concurrent users, each expecting 30 tokens/sec?
52. Human review costs GBP 2 per document and the model costs GBP 0.05. At what
    straight-through rate does automation pay for its build cost of GBP 120k within
    a year, on 100k documents/year?
53. p95 latency budget of 4s for an assistant: allocate it across retrieval,
    reranking, generation and network.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
I. PASS/FAIL PROBES (ask at least 2)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
54. Give me three AI use cases you would advise an insurer NOT to do right now, and
    why.
55. "Can you guarantee the AI won't make mistakes?" Answer as you would to the CEO.
56. What is the single artefact that most increases an AI project's chance of
    reaching production? (Expect: a labelled evaluation set agreed with the
    business, or an equivalent answer, with reasons.)
57. In two minutes: what do you bring to an AI engagement that a pure data scientist
    or a pure management consultant doesn't?
